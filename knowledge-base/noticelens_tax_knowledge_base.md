# NoticeLens — Indian GST + Income Tax Knowledge Base
## Version: 2026-09-11
## Purpose: Retrieval/reference data for NoticeLens
## Source policy: official Indian government material first

IMPORTANT:
- This is reference knowledge for explaining notices, not the full text of legislation.
- Never invent a provision, rate, threshold, deadline, amount, or legal conclusion.
- Applicability must be determined by the relevant financial year / tax year / assessment year / GST tax period and effective date.
- For an uploaded notice, the document itself is the primary source for facts. This knowledge base explains the law and terminology.
- Where a section has multiple amendments, use the version applicable to the relevant period.
- Where current law and legacy law differ, identify the governing law before answering.

======================================================================
A. UNIVERSAL NOTICE-ANALYSIS RULES
======================================================================

1. Extract before interpreting:
   - issuing authority
   - document type
   - notice/reference/DIN number
   - notice/order date
   - taxpayer name/PAN/GSTIN if stated
   - financial year/tax year/assessment year/tax period
   - section/rule/notification/circular cited
   - amount(s)
   - response/payment date
   - issues alleged
   - information/documents requested
   - consequences stated in the notice

2. Distinguish:
   - FACT FROM NOTICE
   - LAW / REFERENCE KNOWLEDGE
   - AI EXPLANATION
   - PRACTICAL NEXT STEP
   - UNCERTAINTY / PROFESSIONAL REVIEW

3. Never infer a deadline from a generic statutory period when the notice itself supplies a date unless the calculation is necessary and the governing provision is verified.

4. Never say a taxpayer committed fraud merely because a notice cites a fraud-related provision.

5. If a document is an order rather than a notice, explain that it records a departmental determination rather than merely an information request.

B. GST KNOWLEDGE BASE
======================================================================

B1. GST LAW STACK
- Central Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (CGST Act)
- Central Goods and Services Tax Rules, 2017 (CGST Rules)
- Integrated Goods and Services Tax Act, 2017 (IGST Act)
- IGST Rules
- State/UT GST law where relevant
- Compensation Cess law where relevant
- Notifications, circulars, orders and clarifications
- GST forms/procedures on the common portal

For an intra-State supply, CGST + applicable SGST/UTGST generally operate.
For an inter-State supply, IGST generally applies.
Place-of-supply rules determine the character of supply.

B2. CORE DEFINITIONS (CGST ACT S.2)
Important concepts:
- Aggregate turnover: all-India aggregate value under the same PAN, including taxable, exempt, export and inter-State supplies, excluding specified taxes and inward RCM supplies.
- Business: broad definition including trade, commerce, manufacture, profession, vocation and related activities, including specified activities by government/public authorities.
- Capital goods: goods capitalised in books and used/intended for business.
- Casual taxable person: occasional supply in a State/UT without fixed place of business there.
- Composite supply: two or more taxable supplies naturally bundled and supplied together, with a principal supply.
- Consideration: payment/act/forbearance linked to supply, subject to statutory exclusions.
- Exempt supply: nil-rated, wholly exempt or non-taxable supply within the statutory definition.
- Goods: movable property other than money and securities; includes actionable claims/certain specified items.
- Input: goods other than capital goods used/intended for business.
- Input service: service used/intended for business.
- Input tax: CGST/SGST/UTGST/IGST charged on inward supplies and specified import tax.
- ITC: credit of eligible input tax subject to statutory conditions.
- Mixed supply: two or more individual supplies together for a single price that are not a composite supply.
- Non-taxable supply: supply not leviable to tax under CGST/IGST.
- Output tax: tax chargeable on taxable outward supplies, excluding RCM tax.
- Recipient: generally person liable to pay consideration; special rules apply where consideration is absent.
- Registered person: person registered under GST.
- Reverse charge: recipient becomes liable to pay tax instead of supplier in notified circumstances.
- Supplier: person supplying goods/services.
- Taxable supply: supply of goods/services leviable to tax.
- Turnover in a State/UT and related expressions must be read with statutory definitions.

B3. SUPPLY — CGST S.7
Core rule:
A "supply" is the taxable event under GST and covers specified forms of supply made for consideration in the course or furtherance of business, subject to the statutory scope and schedules.

Key points:
- Supply includes sale, transfer, barter, exchange, licence, rental, lease or disposal when statutory conditions are met.
- Import of services for consideration, whether or not in business, is specifically included.
- Schedule I contains specified supplies even without consideration.
- Schedule II classifies specified activities as supply of goods or supply of services.
- Schedule III identifies specified activities that are neither supply of goods nor supply of services.

NoticeLens should ask:
- What was supplied?
- Was there consideration?
- Was it in the course/furtherance of business?
- Is it covered by Schedule I?
- Is it excluded by Schedule III?
- Is it composite or mixed?

B4. COMPOSITE AND MIXED SUPPLY — CGST S.8
- Composite supply is taxed as the principal supply.
- Mixed supply is taxed at the highest rate applicable to any item in the mixed supply.
Do not classify merely because several items appear on one invoice; facts and natural bundling matter.

B5. SCHEDULE I — SUPPLIES WITHOUT CONSIDERATION
Common categories include:
- permanent transfer/disposal of business assets where ITC has been availed, subject to statutory conditions
- supplies between related persons or distinct persons in the course/furtherance of business
- specified principal-agent transactions
- import of services from a related person or own establishment outside India in course/furtherance of business, subject to conditions

B6. SCHEDULE II
Classifies specified activities, including:
- transfer of title in goods -> goods
- transfer of right in goods without transfer of title -> services
- lease/licence to occupy land -> services
- renting of immovable property -> services
- treatment/process applied to another person's goods -> services
- transfer/use of business assets in specified cases
- supply of goods as part of service/restaurant etc. subject to statutory entries

B7. SCHEDULE III
Certain activities are outside supply, including examples such as:
- services by employee to employer in employment
- functions of courts/tribunals
- funeral/burial/crematorium services
- sale of land
- specified actionable claims
Always apply the exact statutory entry and current amendment.

B8. LEVY — CGST S.9
- CGST is levied on intra-State supplies except specified exempt supplies.
- Reverse charge applies to notified categories.
- Composition levy is under S.10.
- Specific exemptions/rates derive from notifications.

Do not hard-code a universal GST rate. GST rates are notification-driven.

B9. COMPOSITION — CGST S.10
Knowledge points:
- Simplified levy for eligible taxpayers subject to prescribed turnover and conditions.
- Restricted from specified activities/supplies.
- Composition taxpayer generally cannot collect tax from recipient as normal registered supplier and cannot avail normal ITC, subject to law.
- Separate provisions apply to special category States, service suppliers and certain notified classes.
- Exit/ineligibility can trigger tax/ITC consequences.
- Relevant return/payment forms and rules must be considered.

B10. TIME OF SUPPLY — CGST S.12 (GOODS), S.13 (SERVICES), S.14 (CHANGE IN RATE)
Goods:
- General rule uses earlier of relevant invoice/payment events, subject to detailed statutory rules.
Services:
- Timing depends on invoice issuance, provision, payment and other prescribed situations.
Reverse charge:
- Special timing rules apply.
Change in rate:
- S.14 coordinates time of supply with rate change.

Never give a date without checking the exact transaction facts.

B11. VALUE OF SUPPLY — CGST S.15
General principle:
Transaction value is the value where supplier and recipient are not related and price is sole consideration, subject to statutory inclusions/exclusions.
Potential inclusions:
- taxes/duties other than GST
- incidental expenses
- supplier liabilities incurred by recipient
- interest/late fee/penalty for delayed payment
- subsidies directly linked to price, except specified government subsidies
Discount treatment:
- eligible discounts can be excluded when statutory conditions are satisfied.
Related/distinct-person supplies may require valuation rules.

B12. ITC — CGST S.16
Core eligibility:
A registered person may avail eligible ITC on inward supplies used/intended for business, subject to statutory conditions and restrictions.

Major conditions:
- possession of prescribed tax document
- receipt of goods/services
- tax charged has been paid to Government, subject to prescribed mechanisms
- return furnished under S.39
- time limit for taking ITC subject to statutory exceptions/amendments
- special rules where goods are received in lots, bill-to/ship-to, etc.

Late-payment/reversal:
- Where recipient does not pay supplier (value + tax) within the statutory period, proportionate credit reversal/re-availment rules apply.
- Rule 37 and Rule 37A are important in ITC reversal/re-availment questions.
- Rule 37A deals with reversal/reclaim in specified situations linked to supplier's return/tax payment default.

Additional current-law controls:
- S.16 amendments have introduced special relief/extension rules for specified historical periods; always check tax period and amendment.
- Never state "ITC is invalid" from a mismatch alone; determine which condition is alleged to fail.

B13. APPORTIONMENT / BLOCKED ITC — CGST S.17
Common concepts:
- business vs non-business use
- taxable vs exempt supplies
- common inputs/input services
- common capital goods
- blocked credits

Common blocked/limited categories can include:
- specified motor vehicles and conveyances
- food/beverages and certain personal-consumption benefits
- club/fitness memberships
- employee travel benefits in specified circumstances
- works contract services for specified immovable property
- goods/services used for construction of immovable property on own account, subject to statutory meaning
- goods lost, stolen, destroyed, written off or disposed as gifts/free samples
- tax paid due to specified proceedings/penalties
- certain goods/services received for personal consumption

Use exact statutory wording for the relevant period.

B14. SPECIAL ITC — S.18
Covers:
- new registration
- voluntary registration
- shifting from composition to regular scheme
- exempt supply becoming taxable
- certain stock/capital-goods credits
- credit reversal on becoming exempt or cancellation
- prescribed limitations and time limits

B15. JOB WORK — S.19
Principal may receive specified ITC in relation to inputs/capital goods sent to job worker subject to conditions.
Goods must be returned/disposed of within statutory time limits, subject to extensions/special treatment.
Failure can create deemed supply/tax consequences.

B16. ISD — S.20/S.21
- Input Service Distributor distributes eligible input-service credit to specified registrations.
- Distribution follows prescribed manner and conditions.
- Wrong distribution can be recovered under statutory rules.

B17. TAX INVOICE — S.31 + RULE 46 and related rules
Invoice should contain prescribed particulars.
Relevant documents include:
- tax invoice
- bill of supply
- receipt voucher
- refund voucher
- payment voucher
- revised invoice
- credit note
- debit note
- self-invoice in RCM situations where prescribed
- e-invoice/IRN where applicable

B18. CREDIT / DEBIT NOTES — S.34
Credit note can address:
- taxable value/tax charged in excess
- goods returned
- deficiency in supply
- other prescribed reasons
Time limits/reporting rules apply.
Debit note increases taxable value/tax in specified cases.
Tax adjustment must follow the statutory conditions and reporting requirements.

B19. E-INVOICE / RULE 48(4)
- E-invoicing applies to specified taxpayers/transactions based on notified thresholds and exclusions.
- IRN generation and prescribed particulars are important.
- A document that is legally required to be e-invoiced may have tax/invoice consequences if not generated in prescribed manner.
- Do not state a universal turnover threshold; it is notification-driven and has changed over time.

B20. E-WAY BILL — RULE 138 and related provisions
- E-way bill is generally relevant for movement of goods above prescribed conditions/value, subject to exclusions.
- Consignment value and distance/movement details matter.
- Different requirements apply for principal-to-principal/principal-to-job-worker and other movements.
- Wrong/absent e-way bill can lead to detention/seizure proceedings under S.129.
- Never determine a violation from consignment value alone without transaction/movement facts and period-specific rules.

B21. ACCOUNTS/RECORDS — S.35, S.36
Registered persons must maintain prescribed records and retain them for the statutory period.
Records can include:
- production/manufacture
- inward/outward supply
- stock
- ITC
- output tax
- electronic records
- suppliers/customers
Specific persons have additional requirements.

B22. RETURNS — S.37 TO S.45
Important:
- S.37: outward supplies
- S.38: communicated inward-supply/ITC-related statement mechanism
- S.39: periodic returns
- S.40: first return
- S.41: availment/utilisation of ITC
- S.42/43: historical matching-related framework (amendments have affected operation)
- S.44: annual return
- S.45: final return
Current forms/processes may include GSTR-1, GSTR-1A, GSTR-3B, GSTR-4, GSTR-5, GSTR-6, GSTR-7, GSTR-8, GSTR-9, GSTR-9C, GSTR-10 etc. Apply the form appropriate to taxpayer/class/period.

B23. PAYMENT — S.49
Ledgers:
- electronic cash ledger
- electronic credit ledger
- electronic liability register
ITC utilisation order and cross-utilisation are governed by statute/rules and current amendments.
Interest, late fee, penalty and other amounts have separate treatment; do not assume every amount can be paid from ITC.

B24. INTEREST — S.50 + RULE 88B
- Delayed payment of tax attracts interest at notified rate.
- For delayed filing of returns, Rule 88B determines the relevant amount and period.
- For wrongly availed and utilised ITC, S.50(3) + Rule 88B(3) apply.
- "Availed" is not automatically the same as "utilised".
- GST Council/CBIC clarification has explained utilisation and the electronic credit ledger balance for interest in specified wrong-ITC cases.
- Rule 88B was amended in 2024 to exclude certain cash already lying in the Electronic Cash Ledger on the return due date from delayed-return interest calculation.

B25. REFUND — S.54/S.55
Common categories:
- excess tax/payment
- exports/zero-rated supplies
- inverted duty structure
- finalisation of provisional assessment
- specified international bodies/UIN
- other notified categories
Important:
- limitation
- unjust enrichment
- documentary proof
- electronic filing
- deficiency/clarification
- sanctioned amount vs rejected amount
- provisional/final refund
Never assume refund is automatically due because tax was paid.

B26. ZERO-RATED SUPPLY — IGST S.16
- exports
- supplies to SEZ developer/unit subject to statutory conditions
- refund mechanism may use LUT/bond or IGST-paid route depending on law and notification.
- zero-rated is distinct from exempt supply.

B27. PLACE OF SUPPLY — IGST S.7 onwards
For goods:
- movement-based rule
- bill-to/ship-to
- no movement
- installation/assembly
- goods supplied on board
For services:
- general B2B/B2C rules
- performance-based services
- immovable property
- events
- transportation
- passenger transportation
- banking/financial
- insurance
- government
- intermediary
- OIDAR/online services
- import/export
Inter-State vs intra-State character must be determined with place-of-supply rules.

B28. GST REGISTRATION — S.22 TO S.30
- S.22: persons liable
- S.23: persons not liable
- S.24: compulsory registration
- S.25: procedure
- S.26: deemed registration
- S.27: special registration validity
- S.28: amendment
- S.29: cancellation
- S.30: revocation
Notice types:
- REG-17/REG-18 style cancellation process
- rejection/suspension
- clarification on principal place of business
- liability to register
Exact form/process may change; use current portal process where explaining procedure.

B29. RCM — S.9(3), S.9(4), NOTIFICATIONS
- Tax is paid by recipient in notified cases.
- Recipient must determine whether the inward supply falls within a notified category.
- Documentation, invoice/self-invoice, payment and ITC follow special rules.
- RCM liability does not automatically mean the recipient can immediately claim ITC; ITC requires payment and statutory eligibility.
Common notice triggers:
- unreported RCM
- incorrect rate
- missed self-invoice/document
- wrong time of supply
- ITC claimed without tax payment

B30. TDS/TCS — S.51/S.52
S.51:
- specified deductors deduct GST TDS subject to threshold and notified scope.
S.52:
- electronic commerce operators collect GST TCS on specified supplies.
Rates/thresholds are notification-sensitive.
TDS/TCS credit is reflected in relevant electronic ledgers/statements subject to reconciliation.

B31. ASSESSMENT / SCRUTINY
- S.59: self-assessment
- S.60: provisional assessment
- S.61: scrutiny of returns
- S.62: assessment of non-filers
- S.63: assessment of unregistered persons
- S.64: summary assessment
Scrutiny is not the same as demand/adjudication. A discrepancy notice may seek explanation/reconciliation before further proceedings.

B32. AUDIT — S.65/S.66
- departmental audit under S.65
- special audit under S.66
Audit can examine books, returns, tax, refund and ITC.
A special audit involves nominated/approved professional and specified circumstances.

B33. INSPECTION/SEARCH/SEIZURE — S.67
Power arises only subject to statutory conditions/authorisation.
NoticeLens should not infer wrongdoing merely because search powers are cited.

B34. SUMMONS — S.70
- officer may summon a person to give evidence/produce documents/information.
- failure to comply can have consequences.
- A summons is different from a demand notice.

B35. GST DEMAND — S.73, S.74, S.74A
Historical framework:
- S.73: tax not paid/short paid or ITC wrongly availed/utilised for periods not involving fraud/wilful misstatement/suppression; applies to periods governed by this section.
- S.74: fraud/wilful misstatement/suppression cases for the periods to which it applies.
Current structural point:
- S.74A was inserted for demands relating to the tax periods for which it applies; S.73/S.74 were limited to earlier periods by amendment.
- Never automatically label FY 2024-25 onward demand as S.73/S.74; identify the tax period and current provision.
Demand analysis must consider:
- tax
- interest
- penalty
- self-payment before/after notice
- payment after SCN
- time limits
- adjudication order
- redetermination where fraud allegations fail, where applicable

B36. DEMAND PROCEDURAL FRAMEWORK — S.75
General rules include:
- opportunity of hearing
- adjudication
- order not to exceed notice grounds/amount except as permitted by law
- relevant limitation
- interest/penalty treatment
- rectification/re-determination in specified cases
- consequences of appellate outcomes

B37. RECOVERY — S.78/S.79 and related
Recovery can include:
- attachment/sale
- recovery from third parties
- detention/security-related mechanisms
- instalments under specified conditions
- provisional attachment S.83
Old arrears can be recovered subject to law.

B38. DETENTION / CONFISCATION
- S.129: detention/seizure of goods/conveyance in specified transit violations
- S.130: confiscation in specified circumstances
These are distinct from ordinary demand proceedings.

B39. PENALTIES — S.122 onwards
Categories:
- specified offences/defaults
- general penalty S.125
- principles for penalty S.126
- penalty determination S.127
- detention/confiscation
- prosecution S.132
Do not conflate a demand for tax with a penalty.

B40. APPEALS
- S.107: first appeal
- S.108: revision
- GSTAT framework under S.109 onwards
- higher appeals under S.117/S.118
Important elements:
- appealable order
- limitation
- pre-deposit
- stay
- rectification
- appellate relief
Always use the order date and current limitation rules.

C. INCOME TAX KNOWLEDGE BASE — DUAL-LAW MODEL
======================================================================

C1. CURRENT TRANSITION
- Income-tax Act, 2025 applies from Tax Year 2026-27 (financial year beginning 1 April 2026).
- The Income-tax Act, 1961 continues to govern earlier tax years and proceedings that continue under transitional provisions.
- AY 2026-27 and earlier remain governed by the 1961 Act for assessment proceedings; the Income Tax Department explicitly states that scrutiny for AY 2026-27 continues under the old Act.
- Old Act liabilities/demands remain payable and recoverable after 1 April 2026.
- Rights/obligations that arose under the old Act continue under savings/transitional rules.
- TDS for sums paid/credited on or before 31 March 2026 follows the 1961 Act; from 1 April 2026 the 2025 Act applies, subject to transitional rules.
- Advance tax for FY 2025-26/AY 2026-27 remains under the old Act; tax year 2026-27 uses the new Act.
- For new Act, terminology shifts from "assessment year" to "tax year" in the core structure.

Common:

C11. RETURN OF INCOME — LEGACY
Income-tax Act, 1961:
- S.139(1): original return
- S.139(3): return of loss
- S.139(4): belated return
- S.139(5): revised return
- S.139(8A): updated return

CURRENT 2025 ACT:
- Section 263 contains original return (sub-s.1), belated (sub-s.4), revised (sub-s.5) and updated (sub-s.6) return framework.
Always identify which Act governs.

C12. DEFECTIVE RETURN
Legacy:
- S.139(9)
A return may be treated as defective when statutory particulars/documents/conditions are not met.
Notice usually provides an opportunity to rectify within prescribed period.
Current equivalent must be checked under the 2025 Act.

C13. NOTICE UNDER LEGACY S.142(1)
Purpose:
- inquiry before assessment
- asking for accounts/documents/information
Common requests:
- bank statements
- books
- invoices
- reconciliation
- capital account
- investment evidence
- source of income
- deduction support
- transaction explanations
Important:
A S.142(1) notice is not itself a demand; it is generally an information/compliance proceeding before assessment.

CURRENT 2025 ACT:
- The Department's notified Rules show S.268(1) as the corresponding notice provision for a 142(1)-type request.
- Use S.268 of the 2025 Act for current tax-year proceedings where applicable, not an unverified legacy number.

C14. LEGACY S.143(1) INTIMATION
Computerised return processing can create:
- arithmetic adjustments
- incorrect claims apparent from return
- tax credit mismatch
- disallowance of specified deductions/entries as permitted
- tax/refund/demand
It is different from a scrutiny notice.

C15. LEGACY S.143(2) SCRUTINY NOTICE
Purpose:
- scrutiny of return to verify correctness/completeness and determine tax.
Can lead to:
- questionnaire
- information requests
- assessment order
Do not treat a scrutiny notice as proof of tax evasion.

C16. LEGACY S.143(3) ASSESSMENT
After considering return, evidence and responses, AO makes assessment under statutory framework.
The resulting order may create additional tax, interest, penalty exposure or refund.

C17. LEGACY S.144 BEST-JUDGMENT ASSESSMENT
May apply where:
- return not filed in required circumstances
- statutory notices not complied with
- books/evidence are inadequate in prescribed circumstances
AO determines income to best judgment subject to law and procedural safeguards.

C18. REASSESSMENT — LEGACY S.147/148/148A
Core concept:
Income believed to have escaped assessment may trigger reassessment subject to statutory conditions, information and time limits.
Old S.148A involved specified preliminary procedure/opportunity in relevant cases.
Never state that a notice is invalid solely because the taxpayer disagrees with its reason; validity can depend on procedural facts, limitation, approval and transitional provisions.

CURRENT 2025 ACT:
New Act has a reorganised reassessment chapter. Use official current-law section and time-limit data for Tax Year 2026-27 onward; do not invent a numeric mapping.

C19. LEGACY S.154 RECTIFICATION
Purpose:
- correction of mistake apparent from record.
Examples:
- arithmetic error
- incorrect tax credit
- clear processing mistake
It is not a substitute for a full appeal on debatable issues.

C20. LEGACY S.156 DEMAND
Notice of demand communicates tax/interest/penalty/other amount payable pursuant to assessment or other order.
NoticeLens should extract:
- demand amount
- section/order reference
- assessment year
- due date
- whether already paid
- whether appeal/stay exists

C21. LEGACY S.245 REFUND ADJUSTMENT
Refund may be adjusted against outstanding demand subject to statutory conditions/procedure.
AI should distinguish:
- confirmed/outstanding demand
- disputed demand
- adjusted refund
- response/opportunity where applicable

C22. ADVANCE TAX
Legacy:
- S.208: liability where prescribed tax payable is at least Rs.10,000, subject to exceptions.
- S.234B: interest for default in advance-tax payment.
- S.234C: interest for deferment of instalments.
Current 2025 Act:
- S.404: Rs.10,000 threshold.
- S.405: advance-tax computation formula.
- S.408(2): presumptive-taxpayer one-instalment framework by 15 March.
- S.424 corresponds to legacy 234B.
- S.425 corresponds to legacy 234C.
Interest rate stated by official FAQ remains 1% per month/part for 424 and 1%/3% as applicable for 425.

C23. SELF-ASSESSMENT TAX
Legacy S.140A.
Payment is a mode of discharging liability; governing law is tied to the tax year/assessment year to which the income relates, not simply the date of payment.
Thus FY 2025-26 tax paid in FY 2026-27 can still be governed by the 1961 Act.

- S.194A: interest other than securities
- S.194C: contractors
- S.194H: commission/brokerage
- S.194I: rent
- S.194J: professional/technical fees
- S.194IA: transfer of immovable property
- S.194IB: rent by specified individuals/HUF
- S.194M: certain payments by individuals/HUF
- S.194Q: purchase of goods
- S.194S: virtual digital asset
- S.195: payments to non-residents
Other specific sections may apply.

CURRENT 2025 ACT:
- Section 392: TDS on salaries.
- Section 393: TDS on other specified payments, structured in tables for residents, non-residents and any person.
- Exact rates/thresholds are provision/Finance Act dependent.
- Section 394: TCS.
Official Department guidance says transactions on/after 1 April 2026 should quote the relevant Section 393 table item instead of old numbers such as 194C/194J/194H.

C25. TDS DEPOSIT / DEFAULT
Legacy general deposit rule:
- generally by 7th of following month
- March deduction by non-government deductors generally by 30 April
- specified challan-cum-statement transactions have special timelines
Current Rules 2026 retained the same broad timelines under Rule 218.
Default can attract interest:
- 1% per month/part for failure to deduct/collect from due date to actual deduction/collection
- 1.5% per month/part for failure to deposit after deduction/collection
Current 2025 Act: S.398(3)(a).

C26. TDS RETURNS/CERTIFICATES

- quarterly TDS statements
- Form 16
- Form 16A
- Form 26AS
- TAN
- challan matching
- short deduction
- non-deduction
- late filing
- correction statement

C27. AIS / TIS / FORM 26AS
For old-law periods:
- AIS, TIS, 26AS are major reconciliation sources.
For Tax Year 2026-27 onward:
- official Department FAQ states AIS is replaced by Form 168 as the evolved Annual Information Statement.
Important interpretation:
- third-party information is a lead/reported transaction, not automatic proof of taxable income in the exact reported amount.
- taxpayer should reconcile and respond to inaccuracies.

- 80C: specified investments/payments, subject to cap/conditions
- 80CCC: certain pension annuity
- 80CCD: NPS/additional contribution/employer contribution rules
- 80D: medical insurance/health-related payments
- 80DD: specified disability dependent
- 80DDB: specified disease treatment
- 80E: higher-education loan interest

- 80G: donations
- 80GG: rent paid, subject to conditions
- 80TTA: savings interest for eligible individuals/HUF
- 80TTB: interest for senior citizens
- 80U: disability of assessee
Tax-regime restrictions vary by year.

- Deductions are reorganised; current section mapping must be taken from the new Act.

Never assume old section number = current section number.

D. NOTICE TYPE LIBRARY
======================================================================

D1. GST SCRUTINY / ASMT-10 STYLE NOTICE
Typical purpose:
- mismatch/discrepancy identified in return data
Could ask for:
- explanation
- reconciliation
- payment if liability admitted
The notice itself is not necessarily a final demand.

D2. GST SCN / DRC-01 STYLE DEMAND PROCEEDING
Typical components:
- period
- legal provision
- tax
- interest
- penalty
- grounds
- response/hearing
DRC-01 is a summary of show-cause notice in relevant proceedings; DRC-07 records summary of order.
Do not confuse the two.

D3. GST REGISTRATION CANCELLATION NOTICE
Common grounds:
- non-filing
- business not conducted
- registration obtained by fraud/misstatement
- violation of provisions
Possible next steps:
- reply within stated time
- revocation if cancelled where legally available
- restore compliance/returns
Never advise "surrender" without seeing the notice context.

D4. GST ITC NOTICE
Analyze:
- supplier data
- tax period
- ITC claimed
- legal condition alleged
- whether mismatch is data-only or substantive
- invoice/payment
- GSTR-2B/auto-generated statement and supplier compliance where relevant
A data mismatch alone is not proof of ineligibility.

D5. GST RCM NOTICE
Check:
- nature of supply
- supplier category
- notified RCM entry
- place of supply
- time of supply
- tax paid
- ITC claimed

D6. GST DEMAND NOTICE
Extract:
- tax
- interest
- penalty
- period
- section
- grounds
- payment opportunity
- hearing/response date

D7. INCOME TAX 142(1)-TYPE NOTICE
Core:
- information/document request before assessment.
Response should map every department question to:
- requested data
- source document
- reconciliation
- explanation
Never answer missing facts.

D8. INCOME TAX 143(1)-TYPE INTIMATION
Explain:
- processed return
- adjustment/discrepancy
- tax payable/refund
- interest
- TDS/TCS/advance-tax credit
- possible next procedural step

D9. INCOME TAX 143(2)-TYPE SCRUTINY
Explain:
- scrutiny selection does not by itself prove wrong income
- department can request evidence/reconciliation
- assessment follows after enquiry

D10. INCOME TAX REASSESSMENT
Extract:
- information/reason
- tax year/AY
- legal provision
- notice date
- response date
- reassessment consequence
Need professional review where limitation/jurisdiction/reason-to-believe/approval issues arise.

D11. INCOME TAX DEMAND
Explain:
- source of demand
- tax/interest/penalty
- payment deadline
- outstanding-demand status
- rectification/appeal/stay possibilities

E. RESPONSE STYLE KNOWLEDGE
======================================================================

When a user asks "What does this notice mean?":
1. Name notice type.
2. State why it was issued based on notice.
3. Identify the relevant provision.
4. Explain the provision in 2-5 simple sentences.
5. List exact requested action/documents.
6. State date/amount only if visible/verified.
7. Mention what is not known.
8. Add "not a substitute for professional advice" where appropriate.

When asked "Do I have to pay?":
- Distinguish notice, SCN, demand/order, intimation, and information request.
- Do not say yes unless the document creates a presently payable liability or explicitly requests payment.

When asked "What is the deadline?":
- Prefer the exact deadline stated on the notice.
- If statutory deadline is needed, identify the section/rule and period.
- Never guess.

When asked "Can I ignore it?":
- Do not encourage ignoring an official notice.
- Say that failure to respond can have consequences if the notice requires compliance.
- State the actual consequence only when supported by the applicable provision.
